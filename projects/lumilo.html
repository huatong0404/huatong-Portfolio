Lumilo

When a system suggests whom to help next, how can a teacher know why?

01 / The classroom choice

Several students need help. The teacher can visit one first.

In a team storyboard, a teacher sees several signs of difficulty at once. One student raises a hand. Another has been struggling quietly. Someone else might recover with a quick nudge. Lumilo can draw attention to these signals; the next choice still takes judgment.

Our team explored possible classroom supports through interviews, feature priorities, and storyboards. The question I followed was smaller and harder: what makes one student the next person to help?

02 / Returning to the evidence

A signal does not explain the whole situation

I returned to two earlier studies and reorganized the existing teacher interviews into an affinity diagram. Teachers described students who never raise their hands, and moments when several alerts appear at once. They also drew on what they knew about a student, a task, and the room before deciding where to go.

A raised hand, a long pause, and unproductive struggle do not lead to one universal visiting order. The recommendation needs a reason that a teacher can check against their own reading of the class.

03 / My contribution

From scattered cues to a proposed priority

I translated the patterns into a decision model and a tree. They organize observable signals, student and task context, and the trade-offs a teacher makes before a possible recommendation appears. This is a design framework; its ranking has not been validated as a prediction of who needs help most.

04 / Design direction

The recommendation shows its work

The final design lets a teacher see why a student was suggested. The explanation connects a recommendation to the signals behind it, so the teacher can compare those reasons with what they know about the student and the classroom.

That is the question I want this work to leave open: what counts as a student's need, when is it time to intervene, and where might the system's reading be wrong? The teacher keeps the authority to answer in the room.
